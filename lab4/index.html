What the visualization suggests

The analyzed data are a reproducible, year-stratified sample of 3,000 FPL tweets drawn from 114,466 public records spanning 2012–2023. Cleaning filled missing hashtag and location values, parsed timestamps and engagement fields, stripped HTML from sources, normalized URLs and mentions, lemmatized TF-IDF tokens, and replaced rounded source IDs with collision-free hashes. Every retained tweet was scored with cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest. The D3 bars encode sentiment by color and interactions by tier. Neutral dominates all tiers, but falls from 76.3% among zero-interaction posts to 60.3% at five or more interactions. Positive sentiment rises from 15.5% to 28.4%, while negative rises modestly from 8.2% to 11.3%. Verified accounts are somewhat more positive overall (22.5% versus 18.1%), though only 111 verified posts are sampled, so this contrast should be treated cautiously. TF-IDF lift gives context: positive posts emphasize “join,” “win,” and “good,” while negative posts feature “bad.” RoBERTa labels are model estimates, not ground truth, particularly for sarcasm and non-English posts.

Data and method

Reproducible workflow

1 · Acquire and sample

The source is the CC0 FPL Tweets Dataset. The Python script can download the Kaggle ZIP and takes 250 valid records from each year with seed 401. Both the 3,000-row raw sample and processed data are included in this repository.

2 · Clean and engineer

Pandas validates 13 fields, removes unusable records, fills missing categorical values, cleans embedded HTML, creates engagement tiers, and preserves raw text beside normalized model and TF-IDF text.

3 · Score and visualize

The required Twitter RoBERTa model scores every retained tweet. D3 then combines sentiment with engagement and account verification in an interactive chart.

Reproduce with python lab4/clean_tweets.py. The script, environment requirements, raw sample, cleaned data, chart summary, and TF-IDF terms are all versioned with the page.
