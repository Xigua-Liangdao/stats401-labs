What the bulletin shows

  1. What are the major topics?

    The eight clusters separate arts and media, Chinese language and culture, credit and study pathways, registration and academic standing, history and society, science and computing, learning and student support, and health and public policy. Science and computing is the largest group (166 passages), followed by registration and academic standing (161). The groups are broad: a course can discuss several ideas even though K-means assigns it one label.

  2. Which topics cross formal boundaries?

    Credit and study pathways appears in 46 sections, registration and academic standing in 40, and learning and student support in 29. These subjects recur throughout the bulletin rather than belonging to one chapter. Searching for “credit” finds 127 passages across seven topics, with most in the two policy-related groups. A shared word therefore does not guarantee the same context.

  3. Which sections are most diverse?

    Among sections with at least ten passages, Course Descriptions and Majors are the most semantically diverse. Course Descriptions contains all eight topics across 597 passages; Majors contains seven across 74. Their topic entropies are 2.62 and 2.60 bits, respectively, compared with a maximum of 3 bits for eight equally represented topics. The matrix shows why: these sections spread across subject areas, while many administrative sections concentrate on one or two topics.

  4. Where do different sections discuss similar ideas?

    Study Away, page 75 (p0303) and Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere, page 39 (p0128) both describe limits on transfer credits and restrictions during leave or suspension. Their embedding cosine similarity is 0.923. The first presents the rules in a study-away context, while the second gives the wider transfer-credit policy. Their connection is clearer through the neighbor list than through their distant page numbers.

Design description

I treated a paragraph or short policy block as one passage. PDF bookmarks supply the chapter, section, and subsection, while text positions preserve the page range. Cleaning joins wrapped lines and cross-page continuations, removes exact duplicates, and excludes short fragments, footnotes, and table rows. This leaves a prose corpus rather than a transcription of every item in the bulletin.

I used all-MiniLM-L6-v2 to produce normalized, 384-dimensional embeddings. Long passages are divided into 220-token windows; their vectors are averaged by token count and normalized again. UMAP uses cosine distance, 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15, and seed 401. Eight K-means clusters are fitted to the original vectors with 20 initializations and the same seed. Topic names come from representative passages and their characteristic TF-IDF terms.

Map position represents the UMAP projection, color identifies topic, and circle size shows passage length. Search highlights matching passages, and section and topic filters reduce the visible set. Clicking a point keeps the original passage and its hierarchy in the detail panel. Its five neighbors are ranked by cosine similarity in the original embedding space, not by distance on the screen.

The matrix counts passages in each section and topic. Clicking a cell filters the map; selecting a point outlines its cell. A logarithmic color scale keeps small counts visible beside Course Descriptions. The findings show that policy themes cross section boundaries, while course descriptions cover many subjects. These clusters are interpretive groupings with overlap, and the two-dimensional projection should not be read as an exact semantic map.
