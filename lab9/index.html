Dataset

The course dataset contains the 50 largest economies by 2025 nominal GDP, measured at current prices in billions of US dollars. It is based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025; values may include staff estimates and later revisions. These are total GDP values, not GDP per person.

GDP records are joined to Natural Earth country boundaries using ISO-3 identifiers. Gray regions in the choropleth are outside the provided dataset: their GDP is missing here, not zero. Both maps use the same 50 values.

Design and comparison

The choropleth makes Russia, Canada, China, and the United States visually prominent because their land areas are large. The United States and China also receive the darkest GDP colors. I used a logarithmic pink scale because values range from about $300 billion to $30,616 billion; a linear scale would leave most economies nearly indistinguishable.

In the Dorling cartogram, circle area is directly proportional to GDP, with radius proportional to its square root. The United States and China dominate, together accounting for 45.6% of GDP in this dataset. Germany, Japan, and the United Kingdom gain visual weight relative to their geographic footprints. Russia, Canada, and Australia lose it. Singapore and Hong Kong become easier to locate and compare despite their small territories.

The Equal Earth choropleth preserves recognizable boundaries, neighboring relationships, and relative land areas. It is useful for locating economies and examining regional patterns. The cartogram replaces shapes with circles and shifts positions to prevent overlap, so exact distances and shared borders are lost. It better supports comparisons of economic magnitude, especially when land size and GDP differ substantially.

Linked highlighting keeps each economy identifiable across these views. Color shows where large values occur; proportional area makes their concentration more apparent. Gray choropleth regions have no supplied GDP value, and the circles represent only the top 50 economies, not the entire world economy.
